What the EDA shows

Seasonality: leads peak in March and October (~2,300 a month) and are lowest in Jan/Feb/Dec (~1,720–1,780), with an August dip. March is partly inflated by the 2025 stamp-duty spike.

Stamp duty: the March 2025 spike is mostly resale purchases, roughly double the usual level. Lettings don’t spike.

End of the transactions chart: it drops to near zero after late 2026. That is only because those completions haven’t happened yet, not a real decline.

Lead source: Relocation partner converts at 38.8%, about double the 18% average. Branch walk-in is 24.9%, and WeChat is lowest at 13.8%. The relocation figure probably reflects the type of customer, not the channel, which is the kind of finding the segmentation should explain.

Seniority and speed: conversion is 12.6% for juniors, 17.4% for mids and 26.9% for seniors. By reply speed it is 31.0% under 1 hour, 14.8% for 1–4 hours, 5.3% for 4–24 hours, and 0.6% beyond that. Within the same reply band the seniority gap mostly disappears, so seniors win mainly because they reply faster. A small edge of about 5 points remains under 1 hour. This is observational, so speed may not be the only cause.

Virtual tours: closed leads with a virtual visit convert at 29.0% against 14.2% without. That compares engaged leads with all other leads, so a fairer test would compare only leads that had at least one visit.
